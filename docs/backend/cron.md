# §5 The Cron tick as built

## The Cron tick as built (stage 4a)

`scheduled` → `app/scheduled.ts`, once a minute (`"* * * * *"`, UTC). Steps run in this order and each is isolated: a failing step is logged with a stable `errorCode`, the later ones still run, and the tick ends with an error so the Cron invocation shows as failed.

1. **Reconcile** (always, even with the kill switch off). One read through `jobs_claimed_lease` (`status = 'claimed' AND lease_until < now`, 1 row per tick), then one guarded batch for them. Without `send_started_at`: `retry_wait` with `run_at = now + backoff + jitter`, or `failed` / `RETRIES_EXHAUSTED` + incident once the attempts are used up (a Worker that keeps dying cannot loop for ever). With `send_started_at`: `unknown` + incident `SEND_UNCONFIRMED`. Each attempt that has no row gets one (`outcome` `lease_expired` or `unknown`; its `started_at` is the job's last write, so approximate). Every statement is guarded by the same conditions as the job update (`status = 'claimed'`, the same `attempts`, the lease expired, the same send-mark state): a dispatcher that finishes between the read and the write wins and reconcile writes nothing.
2. **Dispatch** (only when `PUBLISHING_ENABLED` is exactly `"true"` and `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` is set; a missing or whitespace-only token logs `BOT_TOKEN_MISSING` every tick and claims nothing). One due scan for up to **3** jobs, then each job to the end before the next one:
   - the due scan repeats the `jobs_due` predicate literally in two branches (`pending`, `retry_wait`), each `ORDER BY run_at, id LIMIT n`, merged oldest-first. A single `IN` with one `ORDER BY` would read every due row to sort it; this reads at most 2 x n whatever the backlog;
   - conditional claim (SQL above, lease 2 min, `changes === 1`); `RETURNING` gives the attempt number that guards every later write;
   - load the publication's destination (`publications JOIN destinations`) and the post revision snapshot `post_revisions (post_id, revision)` at `publications.post_revision`, through the consumer-owned `PostRevisionSource` port wired in `container.ts` to the content module;
   - refusals before any send: destination disabled → `failed` `DESTINATION_DISABLED`; revision missing, empty text without media, text over 4096, media caption over 1024, unsupported album composition, or stored entities that no longer fit (`validatePostEntities`) → a visible failed state; files over 90 MB in total → `failed` `TELEGRAM_MEDIA_TOO_LARGE` (they cannot be uploaded within the largest send bound). Posts with media use caption length ≤1024; 2–10 photo/video assets may be one media group; animation is only allowed alone. No text is truncated or split. Every refusal writes the attempt row (`outcome = 'skipped'`), incident, `publications.last_error_code` and audit row in one guarded batch and never calls Telegram;
   - `send_started_at` in its own statement, `WHERE status = 'claimed' AND send_started_at IS NULL AND attempts = ? AND lease_until >= now + bound + 5 s`. The last condition makes the lease outlive the call and the outcome write; if it is not met (or `changes !== 1`) nothing is sent and reconcile recovers the job. Only a confirmed mark allows the call;
   - one `PublicationTransport.send` (grammY `Api` without auto-retry, `AbortSignal.timeout(bound)`: 10 s plus the upload of its files at a pessimistic 1 MB/s (decimal), at most 100 s, so a 45 MB video gets 55 s — DEV 22.10.2026 a 40+ MB video cut off at 10 s went `unknown`), never throws: an unexpected throw is also `unknown`;
   - one guarded batch for the outcome (attempt row, incident, publication, audit, then the job update last), guarded by `status = 'claimed'` and the same attempt; if the first write throws, the job and its attempt row are re-read: a write that committed and lost its response counts as applied, otherwise it is repeated once (guarded, so a repeat cannot duplicate). One such retry is allowed per tick; the next failing write ends the tick (the job is recovered by reconcile). If a write is not applied, reconcile resolved the job first and its state stands.

   | Outcome | job | attempt `outcome` / `error_code` | incident |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | sent | `succeeded`, `publications.external_message_id` (first) and `external_message_ids` (all) | `sent` | no |
   | 429 (without `retry_after`, a wait of 30 s; review 24.10.2026) | `retry_wait`, `run_at = now + max(retry_after, backoff) + jitter (0-5 s)`, `send_started_at` cleared | `rate_limited` / `RATE_LIMITED` | no |
   | 429 on attempt 5 (`MAX_ATTEMPTS`) | `failed` | `rate_limited` / `RETRIES_EXHAUSTED` | `publish_failed` |
   | 400 migrate / content / 413 too large / 403 / chat not found | `failed` | `rejected` / `CHAT_MIGRATED`, `CONTENT_INVALID`, `DESTINATION_UNAVAILABLE` | `publish_failed` |
   | 401 / 404 from the Bot API (wrong token) | `failed`, and the tick stops dispatching | `rejected` / `BOT_UNAUTHORIZED` | `publish_failed` |
   | timeout, network, 5xx, unparsable, ok without id, transport threw | `unknown`, never retried automatically | `unknown` / `SEND_UNCONFIRMED` (the reason is in the incident `details`) | `publish_unknown` |

3. **Archive** (every tick, even with the kill switch off): see "Post lifecycle as built". One indexed read of the jobs that finished inside the lookback window; only when it finds a qualifying post, one batch of two statements.
4. **Purge** (tick of minute 30, only when nothing was due): one read; only when it finds expired posts, one batch of seven statements (audit row, five child deletes, the posts).
5. **Cleanup**, only on the tick whose scheduled time has UTC minute 0 (read from `controller.scheduledTime`; no state between invocations). Per table at most 100 rows: expired `idempotency_keys`, old `rate_limits` windows, expired `widget_sign_ins`, and ended sessions (revoked, or past both token and hard expiry, 24 h grace) with their `refresh_tokens` deleted first (no `ON DELETE`). Two round trips (platform tables, identity tables). Detached/staged private R2 media is a separate bounded batch (at most 5 objects) on an idle hourly tick; cleanup never delays publication work. Operational retention then runs only when the hourly publication work was idle and no earlier step failed: `job_attempts` older than 90 days are deleted only for terminal jobs with no unresolved incident; resolved incidents older than 365 days and `audit_log` rows older than 365 days are eligible. Each table is capped at 100 rows per tick. Open incidents and attempts belonging to active jobs are preserved. The indexed, replay-safe deletes do not alter job/publication state or send Telegram messages (migration 0030).
6. **Automations**, after publication work and owner alerts. The five-minute Dota slot is queued on every tick of a minute divisible by 5 whatever the tick is doing (review 24.10.2026: a busy tick used to lose it), except minutes 0 and 30, whose slot is queued at minutes 1 and 31 so the housekeeping keeps its budget; the first idle tick runs it. The queue watches (treasures, Steam signal, game files) are sent first and together, before the feeds are read. Expired five-minute run leases become `unknown`; they are never retried. The two registered Dota automation queues use conditional D1 claims, and pause prevents future claims without cancelling a running operation. Scheduled Telegram publications remain independent. Run history is bounded by migration 0032 and hourly cleanup.

7. **AI video reviews** (migration 0072): on idle ticks of minutes 14, 34 and 54 and only while the query budget allows. One `INSERT OR IGNORE` adds a pending row per YouTube/TikTok video aged 74 h–30 days; when reviews are on and the AI budget gate passes, up to two rows limited by `reviews_off`/`budget` go back to pending. At most two reviews per tick: conditional claim with a 5-minute lease and `attempts + 1`, facts and the same-format comparison always, one cheap-model call only when nothing limits it (`settings.videoReviews` is off by default); a provider failure gives the row back, and the third attempt ends as `model_failed`.
8. **Daily and Monday analytics reports** (AI plan P6, migrations 0076–0077; owner 19.10.2026: a presentation with the AI review). On Monday from 10:00 in the owner's zone the previous week's report (`weekly:<Monday>:v1`, always on, like the digest it replaced; `modules/content-analytics/weekly-report.ts`) is stored in place of that tick's daily one and reviewed the same way. The daily report: on idle minute-2 ticks (its own slot and statement budget, about 32 D1 statements, 36 with two overdue releases), only when the owner switched `notifications.dailyReport` on (off by default) and the owner-zone hour has reached `dailyReportHour`. The step first releases reports still waiting after 30 minutes with the numbers alone (`REVIEW_TIMEOUT`), then stores the day once (`content_daily_reports`, id `daily:<date>:v1`: forecasts scored by the reading near their horizon or `no_fact`, the 6+6 table with each video's status, likes and shares as per cent of views, yesterday's growth and its same-rubric peers at the same age, the channel's four most viewed videos per platform as references, its forecasts in `content_forecasts`) and queues job `daily_review` on `AI_JOBS`. The layout of 22.10.2026 (owner, after comparing with the ChatGPT report; `layout: 2` in the stored data, older reports keep theirs): followers per platform with the change from the day before, the day's views (TikTok, YouTube, and Telegram as the 24-hour growth of the last week's posts), the channel's posts of the day or «за сутки постов не было», one monospace table of every video of the last 30 days (at most 12 per platform) with its YouTube and TikTok copies on one line — short name, age in days, views and the day's growth on each — then one line per platform of the older videos' last week (the reading 3–14 days back, nearest 7, scaled to 7 days), at most two milestones a video reaches within four days at the day's pace (from 100к by the first digit, below it 10к/20к/50к/100к), and the review's «Что нового». Short names (migration 0102, `content_video_names`, one per title key shared by both copies) are given by the same review call for the videos without one (letters only, at most 12, never a name another video of the report has) and never replaced by the AI; without one a title is cut to the column. The day's review says only what is new (at most four facts, no main line, question or steps — those are Monday's), «За сутки ничего нового» when nothing is, and «Новая гипотеза: …» when the day raised one that was stored, else «Гипотезы без изменений» unless a fact names one. Videos of 72 hours to 30 days are read every 3 hours (they were read daily, at the hour of their publication, so a morning «+сут» could be a day old). The consumer (`app/workflows/daily-review.ts`) asks the draft model for the review from the stored, already formatted numbers (main fact, up to four numbered conclusions — six in the week's — each marked «гипотеза» or «похоже», an editorial question, up to two next steps — three in the week's; videos named by key, the message shows titles and links); a conclusion that names a video not in the facts is dropped, a review with a number not in the facts is dropped whole (`AI_NUMBERS_UNVERIFIED`; a text may only use the numbers of the videos it names and of the general lines, review 19.10.2026); AI off or out of budget gives the reason instead. A message still over Telegram's limit without the review loses whole blocks from the end and says the full report is in the studio. Release raises content alert `daily:<date>:v1` (its id dedups a second releaser), sent by the content-alert step: one attempt, a lost answer stays `unknown`, never resent. After a weekly report is released the same consumer weighs the analysis rules (`app/workflows/rule-consolidation.ts`, `docs/ai/architecture.md` item 10) and raises content alerts for newly supported rules and for approved ones that left use; a failure keeps the rules as they were. It then queues job `hypotheses` (`app/workflows/hypotheses.ts`, migration 0086): the analyst's open and supported hypotheses (at most 16; a daily review sees them and may raise one, at most 12 open) are weighed against the stored week's facts, one message «Гипотезы недели» lists what changed, and up to two writing hypotheses that became supported are asked as style lessons («💡 Аналитика подтверждает…», buttons `sp:`), so only «Учитывать» makes them rules; one consumer call of at most 50 D1 statements, a redelivered job changes nothing.
9. **Scripts of new videos** (owner 19.10.2026, migration 0078): on idle minute-9 ticks, the YouTube and TikTok videos of the last 72 hours, one per normalized title, without a script link and not handled before, at most two videos handled per tick (a closed one or a late platform copy counts too; about 22 D1 statements) (`app/workflows/script-prompts.ts` → `modules/ai/script-prompts.ts`). The approved Reels scripts of the 14 days before the video are ranked by shared title words (rubric words aside): a clear match is linked for every item of the video in the batch that stores its prompt and reported as content alert `script:<prompt id>` with «✅ Верно» and «Не тот сценарий» (the link is marked automatic, migration 0083, until «✅ Верно» makes it the owner's; the daily review names it as automatic, and only the owner's links count for analysis rules); any other match is asked with «✅ Да» / «Другой» / «Без сценария» (callback `sl:<prompt id>:<y|o|x|u|c>:<candidate>`, each tap a conditional move of the prompt, so an old button changes nothing); a video with no script before it is closed with no message. The copy on the other platform collected after the prompt joins it and gets its script when the prompt is linked. The messages are raised from the open prompts of the last day that have none, so a message lost after its prompt was stored is raised again. The messages go out with the content alerts (one attempt, `unknown` never resent).
10. **Lost queue jobs** (review 19.10.2026, migration 0082; publishing jobs since 24.10.2026, migration 0110): on idle ticks of minutes ending in 8, just before the content alerts that send them, chat jobs claimed more than 20 minutes ago whose run never finished (the Worker died after the claim; the model is never called twice) get one content alert `ai-lost:<job id>` «Черновик не дописался … Отправьте задание ещё раз», then the job is marked; at most three a tick, about 7 statements.
11. **Style lessons** (AI plan P7c, migration 0084): on idle minute-44 ticks, when an edit or remark of the last 14 days is unread, one `style_lessons` job goes to `AI_JOBS`; the consumer (`app/workflows/style-lessons.ts`) reads up to three, one model call each (within the consumer's 50 D1 statements), and raises content alert `style:<lesson id>` for each lesson ready to ask; a failed call gives its edit back for the next hour. About 3 statements on the tick.

Hero statistics (OpenDota, migration 0031) were removed 14.10.2026; their tables stay unused and are no longer polled or purged.

**Automation Center (migration 0032).** One registry row is served: Dota source polling plus watchlist evaluation (the `dota-hero-statistics` row stays in D1 but is not listed since 14.10.2026). `automation_runs` has a unique active-run index per automation and a unique schedule-slot index; HTTP run-now coalesces on that D1 boundary and never calls publication transport. Each idle tick first converts expired automation leases to `unknown` (no automatic retry), then enqueues due slots and claims at most one run for each fixed automation. Pause increments a revision and marks only queued work `skipped`; a run claimed before pause continues. All automation work runs after publication dispatch and owner publication alerts, and is skipped on a busy/failed publication tick. The hourly idle cleanup retains the latest 100 completed runs per automation. Owner-only routes list the fixed registry, history, pause/resume and queue a manual run; no arbitrary jobs, publication controls, cadence mutation or secret data are exposed.

**Manual analytics observations (migration 0033).** Owner-entered `views`, `forwards` and `reactions` are append-only snapshots tied to an already-succeeded publication, with an explicit supported content age, source label, coverage and actor. The server supplies observation time; the insert, audit row and idempotent 201 response commit in one D1 batch. These rows have no FK to deletable publication records and survive their cleanup. They are never collected, inferred, backfilled, edited or deleted automatically. Same-metric/same-age reads use bounded keyset pagination and dedicated indexes; partial coverage requires an explanatory note.

**Message format.** Telegram text is sent as plain `text` plus `entities`, never with a `parse_mode`: trimmed title, blank line, trimmed body — or only the trimmed body for a post created without a title (`posts.title_in_message = 0`, migration 0063, owner 16.10.2026: no title field; its `title` is just the studio name taken from the start of the body, read from `posts` for every revision). Media captions use the same serialization and entities but are capped at 1024; text-only sends are capped at 4096. The title is a `bold` entity at offset 0; approved-body entities are shifted by `title.length + 2` (UTF-16 units). Trimming clips and shifts entities, dropping any left empty. `link_preview_options.is_disabled = true` is explicit for text. Media bytes are read from private R2 as streams and passed to grammY `InputFile`; there is no intermediate whole-file buffer. A mixed 2–10 photo/video group is one `sendMediaGroup` call, with caption/entities on its first item only. Animation in a group, overlong captions, or unsupported combinations fail before the send mark; there is no silent split or truncation. On success, `external_message_ids` stores every result ID and `external_message_id` remains the first for compatibility.

**Idle tick.** With nothing expired, nothing due and nothing finished lately the tick is three indexed `SELECT`s (reconcile, due scan, archive scan; two when publishing is off), no token lookup, no Telegram call, no write, one log line. The archive scan reads the jobs that finished in the last 15 minutes (48 hours on the hourly tick), so its cost follows the recent publishing rate, not the history. Cleanup and purge are not part of it (hourly only). The test pins the query count and a small `rows_read` over 300 finished jobs older than the window.

**Budget per invocation** (Free: 50 D1 queries and 50 subrequests). Per job: 5 round trips (claim, target, revision, send mark, outcome batch), at most 9 statements, 1 subrequest. True worst tick (1 reconciled claim with an incident, a full batch of 2 jobs ending with incidents, one outcome-write retry with its re-read, hourly cleanup and 2 owner alerts): 28 round trips and 49 statements, at most 6 subrequests (2 sends, 2 alerts and up to 2 «Опубликовано» notices, whose origin and settings reads are shared with the alerts), as pinned in `dispatcher-budget.test.ts`; so it fits 50 with no statement to spare even if every statement of a batch were counted as a query (unverified; the repo counts a batch as one). The purge runs on another tick and only when nothing was due: its worst is 6 round trips and 12 statements (reconcile, due, archive scan, expired scan, 7 for the delete, the heartbeat). A test pins these numbers. Calculation, not a load test.

**Wrong token.** A 401/404 fails the job that met it (`BOT_UNAUTHORIZED` + incident) and ends the tick's dispatching, but the next tick will fail the next due job the same way until the token is fixed: switch `PUBLISHING_ENABLED` off first, then fix the secret.

**Settings.** `PUBLISHING_ENABLED` (var in `wrangler.jsonc`, per environment; anything but `"true"` is off; `/api/v1/health` `publishingEnabled` reports the effective state, switch on and token present), secret `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN`. Cron Triggers are declared per environment (`triggers.crons`: `dev` every minute, `production` `[]`), so what runs does not depend on how Wrangler inherits `triggers`; `vars` are not inherited and are repeated per environment.

## Audience upkeep (as built, migration 0118)

At minute 37 of every hour (purge) and at 12:12 MSK (health) the tick claims a window in `system_state` (`ra:purge`, `ra:health`, 50 minutes) and sends one `rubric_analytics` message to `AI_JOBS`: one statement and one queue send, only when the tick's budget keeps two statements and the heartbeat reserve. The consumer (`app/workflows/rubric-analytics.ts`) freezes comment counts and deletes old comments (≤ 5 × 200 rows), or re-checks the setup and sends the «silent» notice. Neither step touches publishing or feeds the tick's failures. The same way, the rubric analytics (stage 7): `ra:score` at minute 22 of every hour, `ra:summary` at 06:07 MSK (window 20 h), `ra:week` at minute 47 of each Moscow Monday hour from 06:00 (the step stops at once when the Monday's block exists), `ra:learn` at minute 52 of the same hours (after the block).

## Rubric scheduler (as built, migration 0119)

After the publication work and the audience step, while at least 12 statements and the heartbeat reserve remain, one
step `rubric-scheduler` runs the scheduler's tick: its locks, the plan when due (every 30 minutes), then at most two
builds (queued as `rubric_build`), two releases and four read-backs. A failure is reported like any step and never
touches the publication work, which already ran. Details: `docs/backend/rubric-scheduler.md`.
